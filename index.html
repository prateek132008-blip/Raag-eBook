Getting Started

Know the Raag, then play or sing it

A Raag is more than a scale. It has its own notes to emphasise, its own way of ascending and descending, a characteristic phrase and a time of day. Raag Parichay is the introduction to all of that — and swar-lipi is the notation that lets you practise it.

What's Inside

Every Raag, laid out the same clear way

Each Raag in the eBook follows a consistent structure, so once you've read one, you know exactly where to look in the next. You study the identity of the Raag first, then a composition in notation, then practice material.

The eBook is completely handwritten in Hindi — every page is carefully written by hand to keep the feel of traditional music-class notes.

01

Thaat, Jaati & Swar

The parent thaat, the jaati (for example Sampurna–Sampurna or Audav–Audav) and which swars are komal, teevra or varjit.

02

Vadi–Samvadi & Gayan Samay

The most important notes of the Raag and its traditional time of performance.

03

Aroh, Avroh & Pakad

How the Raag ascends and descends, and the characteristic phrase that identifies it.

04

Bandish in Swar-Lipi

A composition with Sthayi and Antara, written out beat by beat with the taal divisions marked.

05

Taans for Practice

Written-out taans you can practise once you are comfortable with the Raag's movement.

Details vary slightly from Raag to Raag — see the free preview pages above.
